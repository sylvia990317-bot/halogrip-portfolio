# 08. How it was intended to work

**Narrative purpose.** Explain the mechanism honestly and clearly: solar collection, heated
airflow, stack effect, airflow through the maize, loading and releasing. Use design-intent language
throughout, because none of this was measured.

**Proposed visible copy**

> ## How it was intended to work
>
> The principle is that hot air rises. The design puts that to work in four steps.

*(Introduction: 16 words.)*

> **Collect.** The black box sits angled toward the sun. Its inside is painted black to absorb as
> much heat as possible, and it warms the air inside it.
>
> **Rise.** The air inlet sits low, near the ground, where the air is cooler and drier. Warmed air
> rises out of the collector and into the tower.
>
> **Dry.** The rising air passes through the shelves. It is drier than the maize, so it should draw
> moisture out as it moves.
>
> **Exit.** The air leaves through the chimney at the top. One low inlet and one high outlet is what
> makes the airflow work.

*(Four blocks: 28, 27, 24, 24 words.)*

> **Loading.** Pull the shelves out and spread the maize evenly.
> **Releasing a little.** Pull out one or a few shelves.
> **Releasing a lot.** Unplug the pipe, place a bag underneath, and pour the maize out of the base.

> "So in theory this should work, but there are a lot of variables."
> Project report, 2024

**Sylvia's contribution.** Not separately documented. No individual claim made.

**Team contribution.** Mechanism reasoning and the airflow and solar diagrams.

**Source pages.** Booklet p.35 (collector angle, black interior, low inlet, rising air, shelves,
chimney, single inlet and outlet, the "in theory" quote), p.36 (loading, releasing small and large
quantities).

**Assets.** `Sun rays capturing.pdf` for collection. `Airflow chart.pdf` for the stack effect. Both
converted to SVG. `talkng to friend.pdf` may be used here or in section 09.

**Evidence classification**

| Claim | Source | Class |
|---|---|---|
| The collector is angled toward the sun and painted black internally | p.35 | concept assumption |
| The inlet is low because air near the ground is cooler and drier | p.35 | concept assumption |
| Heated air rises through the shelves and exits via the chimney | p.35 | concept assumption |
| Air drier than the maize should draw moisture out | p.35 | concept assumption |
| One low inlet and one high outlet maximise the stack effect | p.35 | concept assumption |
| Loading and the two release methods | p.36 | concept assumption, design description |
| "In theory this should work, but there are a lot of variables" | p.35 | observation, direct quote |

> **Language rule for this section.** Every mechanism sentence uses "should", "is intended to" or
> the present tense of a described design, never a claim of measured behaviour. The quote at the end
> is the team's own words and does the honest framing without editorialising.

**Visual treatment.** A four-step diagram sequence built from the two source drawings, with the
airflow path as the connecting element. Steps labelled with real text. The three loading and
releasing actions form a smaller secondary row beneath.

**Animation.** The second strongest case for animation on the page. Animate the airflow path
through the four steps on scroll: heat into the collector, air rising, air through the shelves, air
out of the chimney. It explains a physical process that static drawings only imply. Requirements:
one pass, scroll-linked rather than looping, and a complete static fallback under reduced motion
that shows the full path already drawn.

**Mobile.** Four steps stack vertically, which actually suits the upward airflow narrative. Keep
the airflow path continuous across the stacked steps if possible. Do not pin this section on mobile.

**Open question.** None.
